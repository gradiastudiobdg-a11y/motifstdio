# Kai Makes Do
## Brand system v1 · adopted working direction · 21 October 2026

**Small ideas. Everyday stuff.**

An English-language, silent AI creator account about an approachable Asian man turning ordinary materials into little useful things at home. The audience reaction we want is: **“I could try that.”**

This is a complete starting direction for review, not a claim that the brand name, handles or trademark are available. Character photography in this kit is generated concept art. No Flow production, physical craft test, account creation or publication has occurred.

## 1. Decisions and scope

The user confirmed English branding, international reach, no dialogue or voiceover, simple achievable-looking home crafts, eventual monetization, and an attractive Asian male character who does not look like an older uncle or specialist woodworker. Those choices supersede the early older-dad and woodshop explorations.

The name Kai Makes Do, character direction, palette, typography and core series are adopted as the studio working identity. The publishing cadence and handle remain proposals. On 2026-10-21 the user adopted this identity for the studio itself, replacing the motivational MOTIF direction.

### Three possible directions

| Direction | What viewers get | Strength | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Everyday maker — recommended** | A friendly recurring person making useful things with household materials | Recognizable character, accessible projects, room for later brand partnerships | Requires face and setting consistency |
| Quiet home studio | Beautiful hands-only crafts and tidy home styling | Easier identity continuity, strong object focus | Less distinctive relationship with a character |
| Playful young dad | Domestic mini-problems, silent reactions and light visual jokes | Strong personality and recurring situations | Comedy and family scenes add generation complexity |

Use the everyday maker direction. Borrow only a little humour from the third. The small object remains the hero.

## 2. Naming

### Top five

1. **Kai Makes Do** — a memorable person plus an English phrase about making use of what is available. Broad enough for cardboard, jars, fabric and simple home fixes.
2. **Little Makes by Kai** — clearest warm craft identity; longer as a handle and less distinctive as a phrase.
3. **Just Kai Making** — casual and personal; does not immediately communicate usefulness or home projects.
4. **Kai Makes Home** — stronger home/lifestyle expansion; can suggest renovations or interiors beyond this brief.
5. **One Little Make** — flexible and gentle; the name itself does not establish a recurring character.

Recommendation: **Kai Makes Do**. Read it as “Kai makes things work with what he has.” Some non-native English speakers may not know the idiom, so the tagline and project visuals must immediately explain the category. This is the main tradeoff, not a reason to hide the phrase.

### Naming strategy

- Offer: small home craft stories and visual inspiration, created with AI.
- Audience: people who enjoy satisfying transformations and attainable home creativity, across genders and countries.
- Desired feelings: familiarity, curiosity, small satisfaction, possibility.
- Directions explored: named creator; small everyday outcomes; quiet home creativity; practical resourcefulness.
- Prefer: short familiar words, easy pronunciation, no nationality dependence, expansion beyond wood.
- Avoid: “master”, “pro”, “woodcraft”, “carpenter”, random tech-sounding names, and names promising impossible speed or price.

## 3. Brand foundation and audience

**Positioning statement:** Kai Makes Do is the quiet everyday maker account that turns ordinary household materials into little useful home ideas through satisfying visual stories.

**Internal personal-brand statement:** Kai makes home creativity feel approachable by showing small, understandable transformations with everyday materials.

**The one thing people should remember:** the friendly Asian guy who gives everyday stuff a small new job.

Kai is a fictional virtual creator. His appeal comes from the consistency of the character and the quality of the stories, not fabricated life experience, certifications, real-world test results or customer testimonials.

### Working audience hypotheses

| Audience situation | What attracts them | What the video must deliver |
|---|---|---|
| A renter or young household wanting a tidier space | Small, reversible, affordable-looking ideas | An obvious use without major renovations |
| Someone curious about crafts but lacking confidence | Familiar tools and understandable steps | A process that feels within reach |
| A viewer watching for relaxation | Texture, repeated actions, neat arrangements | A satisfying change with a real visual payoff |

The initial working age range is 22–40, all genders. This is a planning assumption. Do not manufacture survey findings or restrict the creative to men simply because the lead is male.

English supports international comprehension; it does not guarantee distribution to specific countries or higher earnings. Avoid US/UK identity claims, regional slang, currency hooks and fake location signals. Southeast Asian appearance and a normal home can remain part of the character.

### Values and point of view

1. **Useful can be small.** A tidy cable box can be a better episode than an elaborate cabinet.
2. **Start with what is familiar.** Scissors, paper, glue, a ruler and reusable containers are enough for the first content batch.
3. **Show why it works.** The viewer should understand the sequence, even without speech.
4. **Finish with use.** Pens going into an organizer are more convincing than a slow beauty shot alone.
5. **Be clear about AI.** A realistic generated scene is not footage of a tested physical project.

## 5. Visual identity

### Strategy

Make an ordinary home project feel organized and inviting. The graphic system behaves like a tidy handwritten project note translated into clear typography: a small series label, a short useful headline, unobtrusive brand credit. Natural light and familiar surfaces should carry more of the frame than decorative branding.

### Logo and wordmark

Primary wordmark: **kai makes do.** in lowercase DM Sans 800. A small honey-colored square after the wordmark is an optional finishing mark, like a piece of tape. The sentence-ending dot belongs to the wordmark presentation; the platform display name remains **Kai Makes Do**.

Use a horizontal wordmark on a plain paper or ink background. A compact stacked form can read `kai / makes do.` for a profile intro or small card. Avoid a hammer, saw, moustache icon, masculine badge, roof emblem or fake hand-carved lettering.

The profile picture should be a close, friendly crop of Kai rather than tiny initials. The face must still read at a 48px circle. The supplied photo is a starting reference; a dedicated front-facing portrait can be generated after the character is approved.

Three design references, for qualities only: MUJI's restrained use of plain materials and simple typography; IKEA's visual clarity around everyday home use; innocent's approachable lower-case verbal character. Do not copy their marks, signature colors or layouts.

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Paper | `#F4EFE6` | Backgrounds, note cards, light closing frames |
| Ink | `#29332D` | Main text, dark footer chip, wordmark |
| Sage | `#7D8C74` | Shirt/setting cue, secondary fills, subtle separators |
| Honey | `#E8B65E` | Tiny marker, progress accent, one highlighted word on dark backgrounds |
| Clay | `#C68163` | Occasional secondary detail; optional, not required every episode |

Use roughly 70% warm natural/neutral surfaces, 20% ink/sage structure, 10% accents in graphic-only compositions. This is a visual proportion guide, not a quota over the underlying footage.

Use Ink text on Paper for readability. Honey and light Sage are decorative accents, not small body text on Paper. For an overlay on mixed footage, use Paper text on an opaque or sufficiently dark Ink chip. Keep contrast in the actual exported frame; never rely on color alone for meaning.

### Typography

Only **two font families**:

| Role | Typeface | Weight | 1080×1920 starting size | Rule |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Opening hook | DM Sans | 800 | 96–108px | 2 short lines; maximum about 6 words total |
| Series label | DM Sans | 600 | 28px | Uppercase, tracking about +1.5px |
| Step / material note | DM Sans | 600 | 40–44px | Sentence case; one instruction at a time |
| Brand footer | DM Sans | 700 | 25px | Quiet all-caps metadata, not a second headline |
| Studio metadata only | IBM Plex Mono | 500 | 26px | No episode or step counter in video |
| Brand-guide body | DM Sans | 400–500 | 16–18px in documents | Comfortable line spacing |

Hook line-height about 0.98; other labels 1.2–1.35. Use natural tracking for the large headline, not squeezed type. Fit by rewriting first and reducing type second. No stretched letters, outlines, heavy red drop shadows, more than two simultaneous text weights, or script fonts over the process.

Font binaries and their SIL OFL license texts are included in `assets/fonts/`; keep those notices with redistributed font files. No operating-system font installation has been performed.

### Imagery

- Ordinary daylight, natural skin, a mildly warm home, neutral white balance.
- Visible paper grain, normal glue edges, cotton creases and small wear on the table.
- Straightforward medium shots plus overhead and hand details. Use the project as the compositional anchor.
- No luxury workshop, dense tool walls, cinematic smoke, dramatic orange grade or immaculate joinery.
- At least half the running time should clearly show materials or the object changing; the character is not a substitute for the process.

### Graphic grammar

A small square or short tape-like dash introduces the series label. Dividers use one thin line. Optional icons are simple 2px rounded line drawings of the actual materials, with a written label when meaning is not obvious. Skip icons when the footage already explains the object.

Design principles: **object first; small enough to try; one thought per frame; consistent identity, varied stories**.

## 6. Header, title and footer — adapted from the screenshot

The screenshot establishes a useful hierarchy: editorial label at the top, large hook below it, image in the middle, smaller brand/series signature at the bottom. Keep that hierarchy and replace the motivational mood with light home creativity.

### Default opening

```text

──────────────────────────────────────────────

HAS A JOB.

          Kai and the project

KAI MAKES DO / LITTLE MAKES
```

- **LITTLE HOME IDEAS** is the content umbrella, doing the job of “Catatan untuk diri sendiri”.
- **THIS BOX / HAS A JOB.** is that episode's hook. It changes each episode.
- Episode and step counters are omitted from every on-frame layout at the user’s request.
- **KAI MAKES DO / LITTLE MAKES** is the brand/series signature, doing the job of “MOTIF / NOTES”.
- No on-frame AI disclosure cue. Keep truthful fictional AI context in publishing metadata and the applicable upload settings.

### Practical master grid

Canvas: **1080×1920, 9:16**. These are conservative starting coordinates, not official safe-area guarantees. Test each final upload preview because captions, controls and crops differ.

| Element | Placement in master pixels |
|---|---|
| Essential text region | x72–876, y160–1480 |
| Header | x72, y168; series label only, no right-hand counter |
| Header divider | y216, from x72 to x876 |
| Hook | x72, y246; width804; two lines, maximum about 220px tall |
| Object/action | Prefer the central and lower-middle region; compose so text never covers the hands or the craft |
| Optional step note | x72, y300 while the opening hook is absent; fit an 804px-wide maximum chip and avoid the current object position |
| Footer signature | x72, y1440; thin optional line at y1492 |
| Right-side exclusion | Keep important details out of x900–1080 |
| Bottom exclusion | Keep essential text above about y1520; do not place the credit at the literal bottom like a poster |

If the supplied still or generated shot puts the hands in a note zone, hide or reposition the note. The zones are a composition aid, not an excuse to cover the project.

### Timing without dialogue

- First 0–2.5 seconds: series label and hook. Show the material or problem immediately; no logo bumper first.
- Middle: hook disappears. Use a short step label only when it explains a real action, such as `Fold the edge.` Most process shots can be text-free.
- Final 2–3 seconds: show the object in use, with optional `A little more useful.` and the footer signature.
- Footer need not stay on every frame. Bring it in at the opening and reveal so the process remains spacious.
- No word-by-word subtitle karaoke, since there is no narration. One gentle 6–10-frame fade or slide is enough. No bouncing text on every beat.

### Series extensions

| Header | Footer suffix | Use |
|---|---|---|
| LITTLE HOME IDEAS | LITTLE MAKES | Main everyday craft series |
| SECOND LIFE | REUSE NOTES | Giving an ordinary container or material a new use |
| TINY HOME FIXES | HOME NOTES | A small non-structural organization or convenience problem |

Do not launch all three identities at once. Start with LITTLE HOME IDEAS and add the others only if enough distinct episodes exist.

### Hook copy bank

`THIS BOX HAS A JOB.` · `KEEP THE LID.` · `A PLACE FOR THE SMALL STUFF.` · `ONE JAR. LESS CLUTTER.` · `A LITTLE MORE USEFUL.` · `BEFORE YOU THROW IT OUT.`

Avoid claims such as “anyone can do this perfectly”, “takes 30 seconds”, “costs $1”, “tested by dad” or “strong enough for anything” unless the actual claim is independently supported.

## 7. Silent content system

### Accessible project filter

A first-batch idea should use 2–5 familiar main materials, a few basic tools, a small tabletop footprint and one obvious use. The core technique should be understandable in 3–5 visual steps. Assembly may take longer than the video; show drying/setting as a visible time jump when necessary. Simplicity describes the project, not a fabricated completion-time promise.

Start with cutting paper/card, folding, wrapping, lining, sorting, attaching lightweight decorations and simple assembly. Shelves, heavy mirrors, structural furniture, mains wiring and complex wood joinery do not fit the initial promise.

### Content pillars

| Pillar | Starting share | Example | Viewer payoff |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Everyday stuff, new job | 50% | Shoebox organizer or jar pen cup | Familiar object becomes useful |
| Little home upgrades | 30% | Rope detail around a small tabletop mirror | A more pleasant corner |
| Tidy little fixes | 20% | Cardboard dividers for a cluttered drawer | A small annoyance disappears |

These shares are an editorial starting point. Do not manufacture channel metrics to justify them.

### Example episode, exactly 30 seconds

| Time | Visual action | On-screen text | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Loose stationery beside an empty box; hand brings it forward | THIS BOX HAS A JOB. | Cardboard tap |
| 2–5s | Box, two divider blanks and paper laid out overhead | Optional: Box + card + paper | Paper slide |
| 5–10s | Measure and mark the divider blanks, then one clear cut | Cut to fit. | Pencil, scissors |
| 10–16s | Apply paper to box; fold and smooth an edge | Fold. Press. | Paper rub |
| 16–18s | Clear time-jump card to respect adhesive setting | Let the glue set. | Soft room tone |
| 18–23s | Insert dividers into the box in an understandable order | No text needed | Cardboard slide |
| 23–28s | Stationery goes into the compartments | Everything has a place. | Soft item clicks |
| 28–30s | Organizer in use; one restrained satisfied look | A little more useful. | Final gentle tap |

30 seconds is the editorial duration, not a claim about the physical craft's completion time. In real production, time actions from available generated footage and preserve the exact final edit duration.

### Sound identity

No speech, narrated inner monologue, spoken intro or generated mouth movement. Prioritize paper, scissors, pencil, a gentle object tap and room tone. Avoid artificially huge impacts and repetitive click tracks. Music is optional, instrumental and low enough that the material sounds remain legible. Clear the actual rights for sound and music before publication.

A recurring soft double-tap can become a sonic cue when motivated by the object. Do not force it onto every project. A sound can be designed later; do not claim the generated footage includes real recorded workshop audio.

### Authentic-looking AI workflow

Plan from a plausible craft, then define the visual sequence. Keep an object continuity sheet: dimensions, compartment count, color, materials and which piece is being attached. One simple hand action per shot is easier to control than a continuous complex assembly. Generate clean visuals with no text, dialogue or baked-in platform logo; add typography and sound in finishing.

Maintain exact object count and physical stages across edits. Reject floating tools, a box gaining compartments, glue becoming paint, materials changing thickness or unsupported objects carrying heavy loads. Avoid the transformation happening merely behind a hand wipe.

If later using the local studio, Flow footage remains the production source and final video must remain 9:16. The user's explicit no-voiceover instruction overrides the studio skill's default narration expectation for this account. This brand kit contains still concepts and graphic mockups, not finished generated video footage.

## 9. Growth and monetization

### First four weeks — a test plan

Publish three distinct episodes a week if production quality and cost support that cadence. Use the same face, setting and opening/footer hierarchy, but vary the real problem, materials, steps, framing and payoff. Consistent identity should not become near-identical videos with only the object swapped.

- Week 1: box organizer, pen jar, drawer divider. Establish familiar projects.
- Week 2: fabric catchall, cork frame, bottle vase. Test different textures.
- Week 3: mirror trim, pencil tin, cable labels. Test decor versus usefulness.
- Week 4: mini easel phone rest plus two new variations addressing an actual observed audience interest, not duplicate uploads.

After comparable viewing windows, compare completion, average percentage viewed, saves and shares per view, follows per 1,000 views, and cost/time per accepted episode. Treat small early samples as directional. Compare videos of similar duration; replays can make average percentage viewed exceed 100%. Do not set fabricated universal “viral” thresholds.

### Months two and three

**Month two:** keep the strongest two project families, make a new batch of genuinely different stories, and improve the least clear assembly steps. Establish a small reusable character/room reference set if the pilot is worth continuing. Prepare a one-page media sheet with real account statistics once there are meaningful results. Do not claim collaborations that have not occurred.

**Month three:** review production cost against the actual audience response. If there is a fit, test one appropriately disclosed commercial format, such as a clearly identified materials list or a commissioned virtual-character creative. A longer silent build is an editorial option only when the project warrants it; do not pad clips to chase a program's minimum duration. Use real audience data to choose the next phase.

### Revenue sequence

1. **Audience and trust:** establish a repeatable format that viewers watch and save. No revenue forecast yet.
2. **Relevant product links:** consider ordinary craft supplies and materials only where the affiliate program and account qualify. Describe what the item is; do not imply Kai physically tested it. Disclose commercial relationships.
3. **Brand work:** offer original virtual-character creative for suitable home, organizing or craft brands. Make the AI production and actual deliverables clear to the brand; do not frame synthetic endorsements as real customer experiences.
4. **Digital products:** sell only guides whose actual measurements and build methods have been verified, potentially with a real maker partner. Do not package an untested generated diagram as a reliable construction plan.
5. **Platform revenue:** YouTube Partner Program and TikTok rewards are separate eligibility decisions. Recheck current rules and account region before building a revenue forecast.

### Current platform implications — checked 21 October 2026

- **YouTube:** realistic synthetic scenes require disclosure. Disclosure alone does not remove audience access or monetization eligibility. Separately, monetization policies exclude inauthentic content such as repetitive or mass-produced work. Creative planning, distinct stories and substantive value matter; a pretty recurring template is insufficient. Sources: [AI disclosure](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- **TikTok:** realistic AI images, audio and video require labeling. Creator Rewards has its own originality and eligibility requirements, including video-length requirements around a minute or longer and availability in eligible regions. Do not assume a 30-second AI craft clip earns rewards, or that English text changes account-region eligibility. Check the actual account's in-app eligibility; this research has not established that fully synthetic episodes qualify. Sources: [AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content), [Creator Rewards overview](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program), [program comparison](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/how-is-the-creator-rewards-program-different-from-the-tiktok-creator-fund).

The primary strategy is to build an original useful entertainment property. Neither no-dialogue videos nor AI generation alone establishes or rules out monetization. Program eligibility and content review remain account- and platform-specific.
